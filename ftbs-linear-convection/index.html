Why c ≤ 1? The stencil

The dashed line is the characteristic. The true value at the new time comes from where it lands at time level n. The scheme only uses points i−1 and i, so that landing point has to fall between them, which means c ≤ 1.

Diffusion, dispersion and limiters

FTBS and Lax–Wendroff each really solve a slightly different PDE, the modified equation. Its leading error term decides how the numerical solution goes wrong. A flux limiter combines the two schemes to avoid both problems.

FTBS: numerical diffusion

ut + a ux = (aΔx/2)(1 − c) uxx

The error term has an even (second) derivative, like a viscous term. It damps short waves, so sharp jumps smear into ramps, and no new wiggles appear. You can see why from the update uin+1 = (1−c) uin + c ui−1n: each new value is an average of two neighbors.

Lax–Wendroff: numerical dispersion

ut + a ux = −(aΔx²/6)(1 − c²) uxxx

The error term has an odd (third) derivative. It hardly damps waves. Instead, short waves travel slower than a. A jump is made of many wavelengths, so they separate and leave a train of wiggles behind the jump, undershooting and overshooting the exact solution.

TVD limiter: sharp and wiggle-free

φ = 0 → FTBS,  φ = 1 → Lax–Wendroff

Godunov's theorem: a linear scheme that never creates new wiggles is at most first-order accurate. The way around it is a nonlinear scheme. The ratio r compares neighboring slopes. The limiter φ(r) turns the Lax–Wendroff correction on where the solution is smooth (r ≈ 1) and off at a jump or a peak (r ≤ 0). Minmod is the most cautious, superbee the most compressive.

c < 1

All three schemes are stable. FTBS smears, Lax–Wendroff wiggles, and the TVD scheme stays sharp without wiggles. A smaller c makes the errors larger at the same time, because the error coefficients grow and more steps are needed.

c = 1

The error terms vanish, because (1 − c) = 0 and (1 − c²) = 0, and the TVD correction also has the factor (1 − c). All three schemes reduce to uin+1 = ui−1n, an exact one-cell shift.

c > 1

All three schemes are unstable. The true value comes from outside the stencil, so small errors are amplified every step until the solution blows up. This is the CFL condition. See it step by step, starting from round-off, in the FTBS Stability Lab.
