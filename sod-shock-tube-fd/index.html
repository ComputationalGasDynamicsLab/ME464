Three classic finite-difference schemes

Finite differences store the solution at grid points and replace the derivative of the flux F(U) with differences of F at neighboring points. There is no Riemann problem and no upwinding: these schemes use central differences and get their stability from extra terms in the time step. Compare with the finite-volume Sod example and the linear convection lab.

Lax–Friedrichs

Uin+1 = ½(Ui+1n + Ui−1n) − (λ/2)(Fi+1n − Fi−1n)

First order. Replacing Ui by the average of its neighbors adds a large numerical viscosity, about (Δx²/2Δt)(1 − c²). It never oscillates, but it smears every wave, and a smaller time step makes it worse.

Lax–Wendroff (Richtmyer)

Ui+½n+½ = ½(Ui + Ui+1) − (λ/2)(Fi+1 − Fi)
Uin+1 = Uin − λ(Fi+½n+½ − Fi−½n+½)

Second order. A half step to the midpoints, then a central full step. Its leading error is dispersive, so it oscillates behind the shock, like Lax–Wendroff in the linear convection lab.

MacCormack

Ui* = Uin − λ(Fi+1n − Fin)
Uin+1 = ½[Uin + Ui* − λ(Fi* − Fi−1*)]

Second order. A forward-difference predictor and a backward-difference corrector. It is the same as Lax–Wendroff for linear problems, and for the Euler equations it gives similar results with slightly smaller wiggles.

What to look for

  • Lax–Friedrichs is smooth but badly smeared. Its L1 error is about 3× that of the other two.
  • Lax–Wendroff and MacCormack keep the jumps sharp but oscillate behind the shock, around the contact and at the tail of the expansion fan. Switch to the velocity tab and zoom in.
  • Lowering the CFL number makes all three worse: more smearing and bigger wiggles.

Why the wiggles

Godunov's theorem: a linear scheme that does not create new oscillations can be at most first-order accurate. Lax–Wendroff and MacCormack are second order, so they must oscillate at a jump. The classic fix is to add artificial viscosity near shocks. The modern fixes are TVD limiters and upwind Riemann solvers, as in the other two examples.

Stability

All three schemes are explicit and need CFL ≤ 1, based on the fastest wave |u| + a. Because that speed is only reached at a few points, a small violation can survive until t = 0.2. Try CFL 1.3 (MacCormack fails and Lax–Wendroff is on the edge) and 1.5 (all three fail).
